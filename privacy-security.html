6.2 Protecting Your Privacy and Account

You decide who can contact you on Discord and how well your account is protected. This page shows you how to limit messages from strangers and secure your login.

Message and Friend Request Settings

By default, anyone who shares a server with you can send you a direct message. These steps let you decide who can contact you.

Limit who can send you direct messages

  1. Click the gear icon (User Settings) in the user panel.
  2. Click Messaging Permissions in the list on the left.
  3. Under Direct Message (DM) Permissions, click All Servers at the top.
  4. Turn off the Direct Messages switch.
  5. When Discord asks whether to apply the change to the servers you are already in, click Yes.

Result: Server members who are not your friends can no longer send you direct messages.

NoteIf your project group plans by direct message, you can allow messages from one server only. In step 3, choose your class server instead of All Servers, and then turn the Direct Messages switch back on.

Limit who can send you friend requests

  1. In Messaging Permissions, scroll to Friend Request Permissions.
  2. Turn off Everyone.
  3. If you do not want requests from people you only share a server with, turn off Server Members.

Result: Strangers can no longer send you friend requests. Changes save automatically.

NoteLeave Friends of Friends on so classmates who share a friend with you can still add you.

Block someone

If someone sends you unwanted messages, you can block them, report the message to Discord, or both.

  1. Right-click the person's name in the member list or in the chat area.
  2. Click Block.
  3. Click Block again to confirm.

Result: You no longer receive direct messages from that person, and their messages in shared servers are hidden behind a notice.

Report a message

  1. Right-click the message you want to report.
  2. Click Report Message.
  3. Click the reason that best describes the problem.
  4. On each screen that follows, click the option that fits best until Discord confirms the report was sent.

Result: Discord's safety team receives the report. The person is not told who reported them.

Account Security

Two-factor authentication (2FA) asks for a code from your phone every time you log in on a new device, so a stolen password alone cannot open your account.

Turn on two-factor authentication

  1. On your phone, install a free authenticator app, such as Google Authenticator or Authy.
  2. Click the gear icon (User Settings) in the user panel.
  3. Click Account in the list on the left.
  4. Click Password & Security.
  5. Under Multi-Factor Authentication, click Enable Authenticator App.
  6. Open the authenticator app on your phone.
  7. Use the app to scan the QR code that Discord shows.
  8. Type the 6-digit code from the app into Discord.
  9. Click Activate and enter your Discord password if prompted.
  10. Save your backup codes by downloading them or writing them down, and keep them somewhere safe.

Result: Your account now asks for a 6-digit code from your phone whenever you log in on a new device.

CautionIf you lose your phone and your backup codes, you can be locked out of your account permanently. Keep your backup codes somewhere other than your phone.

Recognize common scams

  • "Free Nitro" or gift links: Links that promise free Discord Nitro or prizes usually lead to fake login pages. Do not click them.
  • "I accidentally reported you" messages: A stranger claims your account will be banned unless you contact a "Discord staff member." Discord staff never contact you this way.
  • QR code requests: Never scan a QR code someone sends you. Scanning it can log that person into your account.
  • Requests for your password or codes: No one from Discord, your school, or your group will ever ask for your password, 2FA codes, or backup codes.

CautionIf a teammate's account suddenly sends you a strange link, their account may have been taken over. Do not click the link, and tell them another way, such as by text or email.
